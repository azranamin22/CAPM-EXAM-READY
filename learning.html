Integration Management

Coordinate all project elements to ensure they work together effectively

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Integration Management?

Integration Management involves coordinating all aspects of the project to ensure they work together harmoniously. It's about making trade-offs and managing interdependencies across all knowledge areas.

Key Concepts:

  • Project Charter: Formal authorization document that establishes the project
  • Project Management Plan: Comprehensive document defining how the project will be executed, monitored, and controlled
  • Change Control: Process for managing changes to project scope, schedule, and costs
  • Lessons Learned: Knowledge gained during the project that can improve future performance

Real-World Example:

Imagine building a house. Integration Management is like being the general contractor who coordinates the electrician, plumber, carpenter, and painter to ensure they work in the right sequence and don't interfere with each other's work.

Common Exam Traps:

  • Don't confuse the Project Charter with the Project Management Plan
  • Remember: The sponsor approves the charter, not the project manager
  • Change requests must go through formal change control process

2. Key Processes

Integration Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Develop Project Charter Initiating Formally authorize the project
Develop Project Management Plan Planning Define how project will be executed and controlled
Direct and Manage Project Work Executing Lead and perform project activities
Manage Project Knowledge Executing Use and create knowledge to achieve objectives
Monitor and Control Project Work Monitoring & Controlling Track, review, and report project progress
Perform Integrated Change Control Monitoring & Controlling Review and approve/reject change requests
Close Project or Phase Closing Finalize all activities and formally close

4. Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

Remember:

  • 7 processes in Integration Management
  • Only knowledge area that touches all 5 process groups
  • Project Charter = Authorization (Sponsor signs)
  • PM Plan = How to execute (PM creates)
  • Change Control Board (CCB) approves changes

Key Documents:

  • Project Charter: High-level, authorizes project
  • PM Plan: Detailed, guides execution
  • Change Log: Tracks all change requests
  • Lessons Learned Register: Captures knowledge

Risk Management

Identify, analyze, and respond to project risks to maximize opportunities and minimize threats

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Risk Management?

Risk Management involves identifying, analyzing, and responding to project risks. A risk is an uncertain event that, if it occurs, can have a positive (opportunity) or negative (threat) effect on project objectives.

Key Concepts:

  • Risk: An uncertain event or condition that can affect project objectives
  • Threat: Negative risk that could harm the project
  • Opportunity: Positive risk that could benefit the project
  • Risk Appetite: Degree of uncertainty an organization is willing to accept
  • Risk Tolerance: Specific level of risk that is acceptable
  • Risk Threshold: Measure of acceptable variation around an objective

Real-World Example:

Planning a wedding outdoors in summer. Threat: Rain could ruin the ceremony (negative risk). Opportunity: Vendor might offer discount for booking early (positive risk). You'd plan for both - rent a tent (mitigate threat) and book early (exploit opportunity).

Common Exam Traps:

  • Not all risks are negative - remember opportunities!
  • Risk register is created in "Identify Risks," not "Plan Risk Management"
  • Qualitative analysis comes before quantitative analysis
  • Residual risks remain after response planning
  • Secondary risks are NEW risks created by risk responses

2. Key Processes

Risk Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Plan Risk Management Planning Define how to conduct risk management activities
Identify Risks Planning Determine which risks may affect the project
Perform Qualitative Risk Analysis Planning Prioritize risks by probability and impact
Perform Quantitative Risk Analysis Planning Numerically analyze the effect of risks
Plan Risk Responses Planning Develop options to enhance opportunities and reduce threats
Implement Risk Responses Executing Execute agreed-upon risk response plans
Monitor Risks Monitoring & Controlling Track identified risks and identify new risks

3. Risk Response Strategies

Strategies for THREATS (Negative Risks)

1. Avoid

Eliminate the threat by removing the cause

Example: Cancel outdoor event to avoid weather risk

2. Mitigate

Reduce probability or impact of the risk

Example: Rent a tent for outdoor event

3. Transfer

Shift impact to a third party (insurance, outsourcing)

Example: Buy event insurance

4. Accept

Acknowledge the risk but take no action unless it occurs

Example: Have backup plan ready if needed

5. Escalate

Risk is outside project scope; escalate to higher authority

Example: Company-wide policy change needed

Strategies for OPPORTUNITIES (Positive Risks)

1. Exploit

Ensure the opportunity definitely happens

Example: Assign best resources to critical task

2. Enhance

Increase probability or impact of opportunity

Example: Add resources to finish early

3. Share

Allocate ownership to third party best able to capture it

Example: Partner with expert organization

4. Accept

Take advantage if it occurs, but don't actively pursue

Example: Be ready to use if vendor offers discount

5. Escalate

Opportunity is outside project scope; escalate to higher authority

Example: Strategic business opportunity

4. Risk Analysis Tools

Qualitative Analysis Tools:

  • Probability and Impact Matrix: Prioritize risks based on likelihood and consequence
  • Risk Data Quality Assessment: Evaluate accuracy and reliability of risk data
  • Risk Categorization: Group risks by source (technical, external, organizational)
  • Risk Urgency Assessment: Determine which risks need immediate attention

Quantitative Analysis Tools:

  • Expected Monetary Value (EMV): Probability × Impact in dollars
  • Decision Tree Analysis: Visual representation of decisions and their outcomes
  • Sensitivity Analysis: Determine which risks have most impact on project
  • Monte Carlo Simulation: Computer model that simulates project outcomes

Key Formula:

Expected Monetary Value (EMV) = Probability × Impact

Example: 30% chance of $10,000 cost overrun
EMV = 0.30 × $10,000 = $3,000

5. Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

Remember:

  • 7 processes in Risk Management (all in Planning except Implement and Monitor)
  • Qualitative BEFORE Quantitative
  • Risk Register created in "Identify Risks"
  • Contingency Reserve = Known unknowns (identified risks)
  • Management Reserve = Unknown unknowns (unidentified risks)

Threat vs Opportunity Strategies:

Threats Opportunities
Avoid Exploit
Mitigate Enhance
Transfer Share
Accept / Escalate (both)

Key Terms:

  • Residual Risk: Risk remaining after response
  • Secondary Risk: NEW risk caused by response
  • Fallback Plan: Backup if primary response fails
  • Workaround: Unplanned response to emerging risk
