Integration Management

Coordinate all project elements to ensure they work together effectively

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Integration Management?

Integration Management involves coordinating all aspects of the project to ensure they work together harmoniously. It's about making trade-offs and managing interdependencies across all knowledge areas.

Key Concepts:

  • Project Charter: Formal authorization document that establishes the project
  • Project Management Plan: Comprehensive document defining how the project will be executed, monitored, and controlled
  • Change Control: Process for managing changes to project scope, schedule, and costs
  • Lessons Learned: Knowledge gained during the project that can improve future performance

Real-World Example:

Imagine building a house. Integration Management is like being the general contractor who coordinates the electrician, plumber, carpenter, and painter to ensure they work in the right sequence and don't interfere with each other's work.

Common Exam Traps:

  • Don't confuse the Project Charter with the Project Management Plan
  • Remember: The sponsor approves the charter, not the project manager
  • Change requests must go through formal change control process

2. Key Processes

Integration Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Develop Project Charter Initiating Formally authorize the project
Develop Project Management Plan Planning Define how project will be executed and controlled
Direct and Manage Project Work Executing Lead and perform project activities
Manage Project Knowledge Executing Use and create knowledge to achieve objectives
Monitor and Control Project Work Monitoring & Controlling Track, review, and report project progress
Perform Integrated Change Control Monitoring & Controlling Review and approve/reject change requests
Close Project or Phase Closing Finalize all activities and formally close

Risk Management

Identify, analyze, and respond to project risks to maximize opportunities and minimize threats

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Risk Management?

Risk Management involves identifying, analyzing, and responding to project risks. A risk is an uncertain event that, if it occurs, can have a positive (opportunity) or negative (threat) effect on project objectives.

Key Concepts:

  • Risk: An uncertain event or condition that can affect project objectives
  • Threat: Negative risk that could harm the project
  • Opportunity: Positive risk that could benefit the project
  • Risk Appetite: Degree of uncertainty an organization is willing to accept
  • Risk Tolerance: Specific level of risk that is acceptable
  • Risk Threshold: Measure of acceptable variation around an objective

Real-World Example:

Planning a wedding outdoors in summer. Threat: Rain could ruin the ceremony (negative risk). Opportunity: Vendor might offer discount for booking early (positive risk). You'd plan for both - rent a tent (mitigate threat) and book early (exploit opportunity).

Common Exam Traps:

  • Not all risks are negative - remember opportunities!
  • Risk register is created in "Identify Risks," not "Plan Risk Management"
  • Qualitative analysis comes before quantitative analysis
  • Residual risks remain after response planning
  • Secondary risks are NEW risks created by risk responses

2. Key Processes

Risk Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Plan Risk Management Planning Define how to conduct risk management activities
Identify Risks Planning Determine which risks may affect the project
Perform Qualitative Risk Analysis Planning Prioritize risks by probability and impact
Perform Quantitative Risk Analysis Planning Numerically analyze the effect of risks
Plan Risk Responses Planning Develop options to enhance opportunities and reduce threats
Implement Risk Responses Executing Execute agreed-upon risk response plans
Monitor Risks Monitoring & Controlling Track identified risks and identify new risks

3. Risk Response Strategies

Strategies for THREATS (Negative Risks)

1. Avoid

Eliminate the threat by removing the cause

Example: Cancel outdoor event to avoid weather risk

2. Mitigate

Reduce probability or impact of the risk

Example: Rent a tent for outdoor event

3. Transfer

Shift impact to a third party (insurance, outsourcing)

Example: Buy event insurance

4. Accept

Acknowledge the risk but take no action unless it occurs

Example: Have backup plan ready if needed

5. Escalate

Risk is outside project scope; escalate to higher authority

Example: Company-wide policy change needed

Strategies for OPPORTUNITIES (Positive Risks)

1. Exploit

Ensure the opportunity definitely happens

Example: Assign best resources to critical task

2. Enhance

Increase probability or impact of opportunity

Example: Add resources to finish early

3. Share

Allocate ownership to third party best able to capture it

Example: Partner with expert organization

4. Accept

Take advantage if it occurs, but don't actively pursue

Example: Be ready to use if vendor offers discount

5. Escalate

Opportunity is outside project scope; escalate to higher authority

Example: Strategic business opportunity

4. Risk Analysis Tools

Qualitative Analysis Tools:

  • Probability and Impact Matrix: Prioritize risks based on likelihood and consequence
  • Risk Data Quality Assessment: Evaluate accuracy and reliability of risk data
  • Risk Categorization: Group risks by source (technical, external, organizational)
  • Risk Urgency Assessment: Determine which risks need immediate attention

Quantitative Analysis Tools:

  • Expected Monetary Value (EMV): Probability × Impact in dollars
  • Decision Tree Analysis: Visual representation of decisions and their outcomes
  • Sensitivity Analysis: Determine which risks have most impact on project
  • Monte Carlo Simulation: Computer model that simulates project outcomes

Key Formula:

Expected Monetary Value (EMV) = Probability × Impact

Example: 30% chance of $10,000 cost overrun
EMV = 0.30 × $10,000 = $3,000

5. Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

Remember:

  • 7 processes in Risk Management (all in Planning except Implement and Monitor)
  • Qualitative BEFORE Quantitative
  • Risk Register created in "Identify Risks"
  • Contingency Reserve = Known unknowns (identified risks)
  • Management Reserve = Unknown unknowns (unidentified risks)

Threat vs Opportunity Strategies:

Threats Opportunities
Avoid Exploit
Mitigate Enhance
Transfer Share
Accept / Escalate (both)

Key Terms:

  • Residual Risk: Risk remaining after response
  • Secondary Risk: NEW risk caused by response
  • Fallback Plan: Backup if primary response fails
  • Workaround: Unplanned response to emerging risk

Scope Management

Define and control what is and is not included in the project

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Scope Management?

Scope Management includes the processes required to ensure the project includes all the work required, and only the work required, to complete the project successfully.

Key Concepts:

  • Project Scope: The work performed to deliver a product, service, or result
  • Product Scope: The features and functions of the product/service
  • Scope Statement: Detailed description of project deliverables
  • WBS (Work Breakdown Structure): Hierarchical decomposition of work
  • Work Package: Lowest level of WBS
  • Scope Baseline: Approved scope statement + WBS + WBS dictionary
  • Scope Creep: Uncontrolled expansion of scope

Real-World Example:

Building a mobile app: Product Scope = Features like login, profile, messaging. Project Scope = All work to create those features (design, coding, testing, deployment). If client suddenly wants video calling (not in original scope), that's scope creep unless properly managed through change control.

Common Exam Traps:

  • Project Scope ≠ Product Scope (work vs features)
  • Validate Scope = Customer acceptance (not quality check)
  • Control Quality checks correctness; Validate Scope checks acceptance
  • Scope baseline = 3 components (statement + WBS + dictionary)

2. Key Processes (6 Total)

Scope Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Plan Scope Management Planning Define how scope will be defined, validated, and controlled
Collect Requirements Planning Determine and document stakeholder needs
Define Scope Planning Develop detailed description of project and product
Create WBS Planning Subdivide deliverables into smaller components
Validate Scope Monitoring & Controlling Formalize acceptance of completed deliverables
Control Scope Monitoring & Controlling Monitor status and manage changes to scope baseline

3. Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

WBS Definition: A hierarchical decomposition of the total scope of work to be carried out by the project team.

WBS Key Points:

  • Purpose: Break down work into manageable pieces
  • Structure: Hierarchical (tree structure)
  • Lowest Level: Work Package (where cost/duration estimated)
  • 100% Rule: WBS includes 100% of work (no more, no less)
  • Deliverable-Oriented: Focuses on deliverables, not activities

WBS Example - Website Project:

Level 1: Website Project
Level 2: Design, Development, Testing, Deployment
Level 3 (under Development): Frontend, Backend, Database
Level 4 (Work Packages): Login Page, User Profile, Shopping Cart

WBS Exam Tips:

  • WBS Dictionary provides detailed descriptions of work packages
  • Decomposition = Breaking down deliverables
  • Work Package = Lowest level where cost/time can be estimated
  • WBS is deliverable-oriented, NOT activity-oriented

4. Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

Remember:

  • 6 processes in Scope Management (4 Planning, 2 M&C)
  • Project Scope = WORK to be done
  • Product Scope = FEATURES of deliverable
  • Scope Baseline = Statement + WBS + WBS Dictionary
  • Validate Scope = ACCEPTANCE by customer
  • Control Quality = CORRECTNESS of deliverable

Key Tools:

  • Requirements Traceability Matrix: Links requirements to deliverables
  • WBS: Hierarchical decomposition
  • Scope Baseline: Approved version of scope
  • Variance Analysis: Compare actual vs baseline

Schedule Management

Plan, develop, manage, and control the project timeline

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Schedule Management?

Schedule Management includes the processes required to manage the timely completion of the project.

Key Concepts:

  • Critical Path: Longest sequence of activities (zero float)
  • Float/Slack: Amount of time activity can be delayed
  • Lead: Overlap activities (start earlier)
  • Lag: Delay between activities (start later)
  • Fast-Tracking: Parallel activities (increases risk)
  • Crashing: Add resources (increases cost)
  • Milestone: Significant point (zero duration)

Real-World Example:

Building a house: Critical Path = Foundation → Framing → Roofing → Electrical → Plumbing → Finishing (longest path). Painting interior has float (can be delayed without affecting completion). Fast-tracking = Start electrical while framing (risky). Crashing = Hire more workers to finish faster (costly).

Common Exam Traps:

  • Critical Path = LONGEST path (not shortest!)
  • Activities on critical path have ZERO float
  • Fast-tracking increases RISK (not cost)
  • Crashing increases COST (not risk)
  • Lead = negative lag (overlap)

2. Key Processes (6 Total)

Schedule Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Plan Schedule Management Planning Establish policies and procedures for planning and managing schedule
Define Activities Planning Identify specific actions to produce deliverables
Sequence Activities Planning Identify relationships among activities
Estimate Activity Durations Planning Estimate time needed to complete activities
Develop Schedule Planning Analyze sequences, durations, and resources to create schedule
Control Schedule Monitoring & Controlling Monitor status and manage changes to schedule baseline

3. Critical Formulas

PERT (3-Point Estimate):

PERT = (Optimistic + 4 × Most Likely + Pessimistic) / 6

Example: Optimistic = 3 days, Most Likely = 5 days, Pessimistic = 9 days
PERT = (3 + 4×5 + 9) / 6 = (3 + 20 + 9) / 6 = 32 / 6 = 5.33 days

Float/Slack Calculation:

Float = Late Start - Early Start
OR
Float = Late Finish - Early Finish

Example: Early Start = Day 5, Late Start = Day 8
Float = 8 - 5 = 3 days (activity can be delayed 3 days)

Formula Tips:

  • PERT uses weighted average (Most Likely weighted 4x)
  • Critical Path activities have ZERO float
  • Positive float = flexibility in schedule
  • Negative float = schedule is behind

4. Schedule Compression Techniques

Fast-Tracking vs Crashing:

Aspect Fast-Tracking Crashing
Method Do activities in parallel Add more resources
Impact Increases RISK Increases COST
When to Use Activities can overlap Have budget for resources
Example Start testing before coding done Hire more developers

5. Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

Remember:

  • 6 processes (5 Planning, 1 M&C)
  • Critical Path = LONGEST path, ZERO float
  • Float = Schedule flexibility
  • Fast-Tracking = Parallel work = More RISK
  • Crashing = More resources = More COST
  • Lead = Overlap (negative lag)
  • Lag = Delay (positive lag)

Key Formulas:

  • PERT: (O + 4M + P) / 6
  • Float: LS - ES or LF - EF
  • Critical Path: Zero float

Cost Management

Plan, estimate, budget, and control costs to complete project within budget

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Cost Management?

Cost Management includes processes for planning, estimating, budgeting, financing, funding, managing, and controlling costs.

Key Concepts:

  • Cost Baseline: Approved time-phased budget
  • Management Reserve: Budget for unknown risks (not in baseline)
  • Contingency Reserve: Budget for known risks (in baseline)
  • EVM (Earned Value Management): Integrates scope, schedule, and cost
  • PV (Planned Value): Budgeted cost of work scheduled
  • EV (Earned Value): Budgeted cost of work performed
  • AC (Actual Cost): Actual cost of work performed
  • BAC (Budget at Completion): Total planned budget

Real-World Example:

Software project with $100K budget: You planned to spend $50K by month 3 (PV). You actually completed work worth $45K (EV) but spent $55K (AC). This means you're behind schedule (EV < PV) and over budget (AC > EV).

Common Exam Traps:

  • Management Reserve is NOT in cost baseline
  • Contingency Reserve IS in cost baseline
  • EV is always based on BUDGETED cost (not actual)
  • CPI < 1.0 = Over budget (bad)
  • SPI < 1.0 = Behind schedule (bad)

2. Key Processes (4 Total)

Cost Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Plan Cost Management Planning Establish policies and procedures for planning and managing costs
Estimate Costs Planning Develop approximation of monetary resources needed
Determine Budget Planning Aggregate estimated costs to establish cost baseline
Control Costs Monitoring & Controlling Monitor status and manage changes to cost baseline

3. Earned Value Management (EVM) Formulas

Basic EVM Values:

  • PV (Planned Value): What you PLANNED to spend
  • EV (Earned Value): Value of work COMPLETED
  • AC (Actual Cost): What you ACTUALLY spent
  • BAC (Budget at Completion): Total project budget

Variance Formulas:

CV (Cost Variance) = EV - AC
Positive = Under budget (good), Negative = Over budget (bad)
SV (Schedule Variance) = EV - PV
Positive = Ahead of schedule (good), Negative = Behind schedule (bad)

Performance Indices:

CPI (Cost Performance Index) = EV / AC
CPI > 1.0 = Under budget (good), CPI < 1.0 = Over budget (bad)
SPI (Schedule Performance Index) = EV / PV
SPI > 1.0 = Ahead (good), SPI < 1.0 = Behind (bad)

Forecasting Formulas:

EAC (Estimate at Completion) = BAC / CPI
Most common formula - assumes current performance continues
ETC (Estimate to Complete) = EAC - AC
How much more money needed to finish
VAC (Variance at Completion) = BAC - EAC
Positive = Under budget, Negative = Over budget
TCPI (To-Complete Performance Index) = (BAC - EV) / (BAC - AC)
Performance needed to meet BAC

Complete EVM Example:

Given:

  • BAC = $100,000
  • PV = $50,000 (planned to complete 50% by now)
  • EV = $40,000 (actually completed 40%)
  • AC = $45,000 (spent $45K)

Calculate:

  • CV = EV - AC = $40K - $45K = -$5K (over budget)
  • SV = EV - PV = $40K - $50K = -$10K (behind schedule)
  • CPI = EV / AC = $40K / $45K = 0.89 (over budget)
  • SPI = EV / PV = $40K / $50K = 0.80 (behind schedule)
  • EAC = BAC / CPI = $100K / 0.89 = $112,360 (projected final cost)
  • ETC = EAC - AC = $112,360 - $45K = $67,360 (more needed)
  • VAC = BAC - EAC = $100K - $112,360 = -$12,360 (over budget)

EVM Exam Tips:

  • Memorize ALL formulas - they appear frequently!
  • EV is ALWAYS based on budgeted cost (not actual)
  • CPI and SPI: > 1.0 is good, < 1.0 is bad
  • CV and SV: Positive is good, Negative is bad
  • Most common EAC formula: BAC / CPI

4. Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

EVM Quick Reference:

  • CV = EV - AC (Cost Variance)
  • SV = EV - PV (Schedule Variance)
  • CPI = EV / AC (Cost Performance)
  • SPI = EV / PV (Schedule Performance)
  • EAC = BAC / CPI (Estimate at Completion)
  • ETC = EAC - AC (Estimate to Complete)
  • VAC = BAC - EAC (Variance at Completion)

Remember:

  • 4 processes (3 Planning, 1 M&C)
  • Management Reserve NOT in baseline
  • Contingency Reserve IN baseline
  • Index > 1.0 = Good performance
  • Variance > 0 = Good performance

Quality Management

Incorporate quality policies and ensure project meets requirements

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Quality Management?

Quality Management includes processes for incorporating the organization's quality policy regarding planning, managing, and controlling project and product quality requirements.

Key Concepts:

  • Quality: Degree to which deliverables meet requirements
  • Grade: Category assigned to deliverables (low grade ≠ low quality)
  • Prevention: Keep errors out of process
  • Inspection: Keep errors out of customer's hands
  • Quality Assurance (QA): Process-oriented (doing things right)
  • Quality Control (QC): Product-oriented (checking results)
  • Cost of Quality: Cost of conformance + cost of non-conformance

Real-World Example:

Building a car: Quality = Meets safety standards, runs reliably. Grade = Economy vs luxury model. A low-grade economy car can still have high quality (reliable, safe). Prevention = Training workers, using quality materials. Inspection = Testing each car before delivery.

Common Exam Traps:

  • Low grade ≠ Low quality (they're different!)
  • Prevention over Inspection (cheaper to prevent)
  • Quality Assurance = Process focus
  • Quality Control = Product focus
  • Gold plating = Adding extra features (bad!)

2. Key Processes (3 Total)

Quality Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Plan Quality Management Planning Identify quality requirements and standards
Manage Quality Executing Translate quality plan into executable quality activities (QA)
Control Quality Monitoring & Controlling Monitor and record results to assess performance (QC)

Manage Quality vs Control Quality:

Aspect Manage Quality (QA) Control Quality (QC)
Focus PROCESS PRODUCT
When During work After work
Purpose Prevent defects Detect defects
Process Group Executing Monitoring & Controlling
Example Process audits, training Testing, inspections

3. Quality Tools & Techniques

Seven Basic Quality Tools:

  • Cause and Effect Diagram (Ishikawa/Fishbone): Identify root causes
  • Flowchart: Show process steps and decision points
  • Checksheet: Organize data collection
  • Pareto Chart: 80/20 rule - identify vital few causes
  • Histogram: Show frequency distribution
  • Control Chart: Monitor process stability over time
  • Scatter Diagram: Show relationship between two variables

Other Important Tools:

  • Cost-Benefit Analysis: Compare cost of quality vs benefits
  • Cost of Quality: Prevention + Appraisal + Failure costs
  • Benchmarking: Compare practices with other organizations
  • Design of Experiments: Statistical method to identify factors
  • Statistical Sampling: Select part of population for inspection

Tool Tips:

  • Pareto Chart = 80/20 rule (80% problems from 20% causes)
  • Control Chart shows if process is "in control"
  • Rule of Seven: 7 consecutive points on one side = out of control
  • Ishikawa diagram also called Fishbone or Cause-and-Effect

4. Cost of Quality

Cost of Quality Components:

Cost of Quality = Cost of Conformance + Cost of Non-Conformance

Cost of Conformance (Good costs):

  • Prevention Costs: Training, process documentation, equipment
  • Appraisal Costs: Testing, inspections, audits

Cost of Non-Conformance (Bad costs):

  • Internal Failure: Rework, scrap (found before delivery)
  • External Failure: Warranty, liability, lost business (found after delivery)

Example:

Prevention: $10K for training developers
Appraisal: $5K for testing
Internal Failure: $20K for fixing bugs before release
External Failure: $100K for customer complaints and fixes
Lesson: Spending more on prevention/appraisal reduces expensive failures!

5. Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

Remember:

  • 3 processes (1 Planning, 1 Executing, 1 M&C)
  • Quality ≠ Grade
  • Prevention > Inspection
  • QA = Process focus (Manage Quality)
  • QC = Product focus (Control Quality)
  • Gold plating = Bad (adds scope)

Seven Basic Tools:

  • Cause & Effect (Fishbone)
  • Flowchart
  • Checksheet
  • Pareto (80/20)
  • Histogram
  • Control Chart
  • Scatter Diagram

Resource Management

Identify, acquire, and manage resources needed for project success

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Resource Management?

Resource Management includes processes to identify, acquire, and manage the resources needed for successful project completion.

Key Concepts:

  • Resources: Team members, equipment, materials, facilities
  • Resource Histogram: Bar chart showing resource allocation over time
  • Resource Leveling: Adjust schedule to balance resource demand
  • Resource Smoothing: Adjust activities within float to balance resources
  • Team Development: Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing, Adjourning
  • Conflict Resolution: Collaborate, Compromise, Smooth, Force, Withdraw
  • Power Types: Formal, Reward, Penalty, Expert, Referent

Real-World Example:

Software project needs 5 developers but only 3 available: Resource Leveling = Extend schedule to work with 3 developers. Resource Smoothing = Move non-critical tasks to when developers are free (within float). Team goes through Tuckman's stages: Forming (getting to know), Storming (conflicts), Norming (working together), Performing (high productivity).

Common Exam Traps:

  • Resource Leveling CAN extend schedule
  • Resource Smoothing does NOT extend schedule (uses float)
  • Collaborate = Best conflict resolution (win-win)
  • Force/Direct = Worst (win-lose)
  • Team development: Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning

2. Key Processes (6 Total)

Resource Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Plan Resource Management Planning Define how to estimate, acquire, manage, and utilize resources
Estimate Activity Resources Planning Estimate team resources and type/quantities of materials
Acquire Resources Executing Obtain team members, facilities, equipment, materials
Develop Team Executing Improve competencies and team interaction
Manage Team Executing Track performance, provide feedback, resolve issues
Control Resources Monitoring & Controlling Ensure resources are available and allocated as planned

3. Team Development Stages (Tuckman Model)

Five Stages of Team Development:

  • 1. Forming: Team meets, learns about project, roles unclear
  • 2. Storming: Conflicts arise, members challenge each other
  • 3. Norming: Team works together, establishes norms
  • 4. Performing: Team functions as well-organized unit, high productivity
  • 5. Adjourning: Project ends, team disbands

Example:

Forming: "Nice to meet you all!"
Storming: "I disagree with your approach!"
Norming: "Let's work together on this."
Performing: "We're crushing our goals!"
Adjourning: "Thanks for everything, goodbye!"

4. Conflict Resolution Techniques

Conflict Resolution Methods (Best to Worst):

Technique Description Result
1. Collaborate/Problem-Solve Work together to find solution that satisfies all Win-Win (BEST)
2. Compromise/Reconcile Each party gives up something Lose-Lose (but acceptable)
3. Smooth/Accommodate Emphasize agreement, downplay differences Temporary fix
4. Force/Direct Push one viewpoint at expense of others Win-Lose (WORST)
5. Withdraw/Avoid Retreat from conflict situation Lose-Lose (delays resolution)

Exam Tips:

  • Collaborate is ALWAYS the best approach (win-win)
  • Force/Direct is WORST (creates resentment)
  • Compromise = Both parties give something up
  • Withdraw = Avoiding the issue (not solving it)

5. Power Types

Types of Power:

  • Formal/Legitimate: Based on position (PM authority)
  • Reward: Ability to give rewards (bonuses, recognition)
  • Penalty/Coercive: Ability to punish (least effective)
  • Expert: Based on knowledge/skills (very effective)
  • Referent: Based on respect/charisma (most effective)

Power Tips:

  • Referent power = Most effective (people follow willingly)
  • Penalty power = Least effective (creates fear)
  • Expert power = Very effective (based on knowledge)
  • PM should rely on expert and referent power

6. Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

Remember:

  • 6 processes (2 Planning, 3 Executing, 1 M&C)
  • Resource Leveling = CAN extend schedule
  • Resource Smoothing = Does NOT extend schedule
  • Tuckman: Form → Storm → Norm → Perform → Adjourn
  • Best conflict resolution: Collaborate (win-win)
  • Worst conflict resolution: Force (win-lose)

Power Types (Best to Worst):

  • 1. Referent (respect/charisma)
  • 2. Expert (knowledge)
  • 3. Formal (position)
  • 4. Reward (incentives)
  • 5. Penalty (punishment)

Communication Management

Ensure timely and appropriate generation, collection, and distribution of project information

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Communication Management?

Communication Management includes processes to ensure timely and appropriate planning, collection, creation, distribution, storage, retrieval, and disposition of project information.

Key Concepts:

  • Communication Channels: n(n-1)/2 where n = number of people
  • Communication Methods: Interactive, Push, Pull
  • Communication Models: Sender, Encoding, Medium, Decoding, Receiver, Feedback, Noise
  • Effective Listening: Active listening, asking questions, acknowledging
  • Communication Barriers: Language, culture, distance, technology
  • 90% of PM Time: Spent on communication

Communication Channels Formula:

Channels = n(n-1)/2
where n = number of people

Example: Team of 5 people
Channels = 5(5-1)/2 = 5×4/2 = 10 communication channels

Example: Add 1 person (now 6 total)
Channels = 6(6-1)/2 = 6×5/2 = 15 channels (5 more channels!)

Real-World Example:

Project with 4 team members has 6 communication channels. Add 1 new member (5 total) = 10 channels (4 more!). This shows why larger teams need more structured communication. Interactive = Video call (real-time). Push = Email (sent to recipients). Pull = Wiki/portal (people access when needed).

Common Exam Traps:

  • Memorize formula: n(n-1)/2
  • PM spends 90% of time communicating
  • Interactive = Most effective (real-time feedback)
  • Formal written = Most important for complex issues
  • Noise = Anything that interferes with communication

2. Key Processes (3 Total)

Communication Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Plan Communications Management Planning Develop approach for project communications
Manage Communications Executing Create, collect, distribute, store, and retrieve information
Monitor Communications Monitoring & Controlling Ensure information needs are met

3. Communication Methods

Three Communication Methods:

Method Description Examples Best For
Interactive Real-time, multidirectional exchange Meetings, phone calls, video conferences Complex discussions, immediate feedback
Push Sent to specific recipients Emails, memos, reports, letters Distributing information to many people
Pull Recipients access at their convenience Intranet, wikis, knowledge repositories Large volumes, self-service access

Method Tips:

  • Interactive = Most effective (real-time feedback)
  • Push = Good for ensuring receipt
  • Pull = Good for large volumes of info
  • Choose method based on urgency and complexity

4. Communication Model

Basic Communication Model Components:

  • Sender: Person transmitting the message
  • Encoding: Converting thoughts into language/symbols
  • Message: Output of encoding
  • Medium: Method used to convey message (email, verbal, etc.)
  • Noise: Anything that interferes with transmission
  • Decoding: Receiver interprets the message
  • Receiver: Person receiving the message
  • Feedback: Response from receiver to sender

Example:

Sender: PM wants to communicate deadline
Encoding: PM writes "Project due March 15"
Medium: Email
Noise: Email goes to spam, or unclear wording
Receiver: Team member
Decoding: Team member reads and understands
Feedback: Team member replies "Got it, thanks!"

5. Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

Remember:

  • 3 processes (1 Planning, 1 Executing, 1 M&C)
  • Channels = n(n-1)/2

Procurement Management

Purchase or acquire products, services, or results from outside the project team

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Procurement Management?

Procurement Management includes processes to purchase or acquire products, services, or results needed from outside the project team.

Key Concepts:

  • Buyer: Organization purchasing (customer)
  • Seller: Organization providing goods/services (vendor)
  • Contract: Legal agreement between buyer and seller
  • Procurement Documents: RFP, RFQ, RFI, IFB
  • Source Selection: Choosing the best seller
  • Make-or-Buy: Decision to produce internally or purchase
  • Claims Administration: Managing contract disputes

Real-World Example:

Building a website: Make-or-Buy decision = Build in-house or hire agency? If hiring, send RFP (Request for Proposal) to agencies. Review proposals, select best vendor using source selection criteria. Sign contract. Monitor work and manage any claims if disputes arise. Finally, close procurement when work is complete.

Common Exam Traps:

  • Buyer = Customer (the one paying)
  • Seller = Vendor (the one providing)
  • Contract is legally binding
  • Changes must go through formal change control
  • Procurement closes before project closes

2. Key Processes (3 Total)

Procurement Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Plan Procurement Management Planning Document procurement decisions and approach
Conduct Procurements Executing Obtain seller responses, select seller, award contract
Control Procurements Monitoring & Controlling Manage relationships, monitor performance, make changes

3. Contract Types

Three Main Contract Types:

Contract Type Description Risk to Buyer Risk to Seller
Fixed Price (FP/FFP) Set price for defined scope LOW HIGH
Cost Reimbursable (CR/CPFF) Pay actual costs plus fee HIGH LOW
Time & Materials (T&M) Pay for time and materials used MEDIUM MEDIUM

Contract Type Details:

  • Fixed Price (FP):
    • FFP = Firm Fixed Price (most common)
    • FPIF = Fixed Price Incentive Fee
    • Best when scope is well-defined
    • Seller bears cost risk
  • Cost Reimbursable (CR):
    • CPFF = Cost Plus Fixed Fee
    • CPIF = Cost Plus Incentive Fee
    • CPAF = Cost Plus Award Fee
    • Best when scope is uncertain
    • Buyer bears cost risk
  • Time & Materials (T&M):
    • Hybrid of FP and CR
    • Good for staff augmentation
    • Shared risk

Contract Tips:

  • Fixed Price = LOW risk to buyer, HIGH risk to seller
  • Cost Reimbursable = HIGH risk to buyer, LOW risk to seller
  • Use FP when scope is clear
  • Use CR when scope is uncertain
  • Incentive contracts align buyer and seller goals

4. Procurement Documents

Common Procurement Documents:

  • RFP (Request for Proposal): Detailed proposal requested, includes price and approach
  • RFQ (Request for Quotation): Price quote requested for specific items
  • RFI (Request for Information): General information about vendor capabilities
  • IFB (Invitation for Bid): Sealed bids, lowest price typically wins
  • SOW (Statement of Work): Describes work to be performed
  • Terms & Conditions: Legal requirements and obligations

When to Use Each:

RFI: "Tell us about your company and services"
RFQ: "How much for 100 laptops?"
RFP: "Propose how you'll build our website, including approach and cost"
IFB: "Submit sealed bid for construction project"

5. Source Selection Criteria

Common Selection Criteria:

  • Price/Cost: Total cost of proposal
  • Technical Capability: Ability to deliver
  • Past Performance: Track record and references
  • Financial Capacity: Financial stability
  • Management Approach: How they'll manage the work
  • Warranty: Guarantees provided
  • Production Capacity: Ability to meet schedule

Selection Tips:

  • Price is important but not the only factor
  • Consider total value, not just lowest cost
  • Past performance is strong indicator
  • Use weighted scoring for objective evaluation

6. Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

Remember:

  • 3 processes (1 Planning, 1 Executing, 1 M&C)
  • Buyer = Customer (paying)
  • Seller = Vendor (providing)
  • Contract = Legally binding
  • Procurement closes BEFORE project closes

Contract Risk:

  • Fixed Price: Low buyer risk, High seller risk
  • Cost Reimbursable: High buyer risk, Low seller risk
  • T&M: Medium risk for both

Documents:

  • RFI: Information about capabilities
  • RFQ: Price quote
  • RFP: Detailed proposal
  • IFB: Sealed bids

Stakeholder Management

Identify stakeholders and manage their engagement throughout the project

1. Overview & Key Concepts

What is Project Stakeholder Management?

Stakeholder Management includes processes to identify people, groups, or organizations that could impact or be impacted by the project, and to develop strategies for engaging them.

Key Concepts:

  • Stakeholder: Anyone affected by or who can affect the project
  • Stakeholder Register: Document listing all stakeholders
  • Power/Interest Grid: Classify stakeholders by power and interest
  • Engagement Levels: Unaware, Resistant, Neutral, Supportive, Leading
  • Stakeholder Engagement: Working with stakeholders to meet needs
  • Influence: Ability to affect project decisions

Real-World Example:

Building a new office: Stakeholders = Employees (users), CEO (sponsor), IT team (implementers), neighbors (affected), city (permits). Power/Interest Grid: CEO = High power, high interest (manage closely). Neighbors = Low power, high interest (keep informed). Use stakeholder register to track all stakeholders and their needs.

Common Exam Traps:

  • Identify stakeholders EARLY (not just at start)
  • Stakeholders can be positive or negative
  • Manage expectations proactively
  • High power + high interest = Manage closely
  • Engagement is ongoing, not one-time

2. Key Processes (4 Total)

Stakeholder Management Processes:

Process Process Group Description
Identify Stakeholders Initiating Identify people/organizations affected by project
Plan Stakeholder Engagement Planning Develop strategies to engage stakeholders
Manage Stakeholder Engagement Executing Communicate and work with stakeholders
Monitor Stakeholder Engagement Monitoring & Controlling Monitor relationships and adjust strategies

3. Power/Interest Grid

Stakeholder Classification:

Power/Interest Strategy Example
High Power, High Interest Manage Closely Project sponsor, key executives
High Power, Low Interest Keep Satisfied Senior management not directly involved
Low Power, High Interest Keep Informed End users, affected employees
Low Power, Low Interest Monitor Peripheral stakeholders

Grid Tips:

  • High Power + High Interest = Most attention (manage closely)
  • High Power + Low Interest = Keep satisfied (don't bore them)
  • Low Power + High Interest = Keep informed (they care)
  • Low Power + Low Interest = Monitor (minimal effort)

4. Engagement Levels

Five Stakeholder Engagement Levels:

  • Unaware: Unaware of project and potential impacts
  • Resistant: Aware but resistant to change
  • Neutral: Aware but neither supportive nor resistant
  • Supportive: Aware and supportive of project
  • Leading: Aware and actively engaged in ensuring success

Example:

Current State: Department head is Neutral about new system
Desired State: Move to Supportive
Strategy: Show benefits, involve in decisions, address concerns
Goal: Increase engagement level to achieve project success

Engagement Tips:

  • Assess current and desired engagement levels
  • Develop strategies to move stakeholders to desired level
  • Leading stakeholders are champions
  • Address resistance early
  • Monitor and adjust strategies continuously

5. Stakeholder Register

Stakeholder Register Contents:

  • Identification: Name, role, contact information
  • Assessment: Requirements, expectations, influence
  • Classification: Internal/external, supporter/resistor
  • Power/Interest: Level of authority and interest
  • Engagement Strategy: How to engage each stakeholder

Register Tips:

  • Created in Identify Stakeholders process
  • Updated throughout project
  • Contains sensitive information (control access)
  • Used to plan communication and engagement

6. Quick Revision Cheat Sheet

Remember:

  • 4 processes (1 Initiating, 1 Planning, 1 Executing, 1 M&C)
  • Identify stakeholders EARLY and CONTINUOUSLY
  • Stakeholder = Anyone who affects or is affected
  • Manage expectations proactively
  • Engagement is ongoing process

Power/Interest Grid:

  • High/High: Manage Closely
  • High/Low: Keep Satisfied
  • Low/High: Keep Informed
  • Low/Low: Monitor

Engagement Levels:

  • Unaware → Resistant → Neutral → Supportive → Leading
